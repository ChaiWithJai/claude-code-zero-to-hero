## Build a seven-day daily-driver experiment

Choose three specific opportunities in the next seven days. Attach each to an existing situation: after a meeting, before a purchase comparison, or when organizing notes. Write the smallest useful result, the source material, and when you will inspect it.

Define a stop rule. If the result invents facts, needs more correction than the task is worth, or requires access you do not have, stop, narrow, or switch to an approved fallback. This is purposeful use, not a streak you must maintain.

At the end of the week, compare motivation, performance, and actual use separately. A strong plan is evidence of readiness. Repeated useful executions are evidence of adoption. Do not claim the latter before it happens.

## Show your world and choose the next orbit

Give a two-minute walkthrough: what matters at the center, what your sidekick does, what it can access, how you checked a result, and where your model changed. Show one artifact rather than a feature list. You may share a sanitized version or keep the binder private.

Use the capability record to distinguish explained, analyzed from supplied examples, executed with guidance, and executed independently. These are different achievements. A learner who records a blocker accurately has learned something important, even if the execution checkpoint remains pending.

Choose your next orbit from evidence. It might be a browser lab, a reusable instruction, or another personal task. Keep advanced engineering and training topics available in the appendix without making them the definition of success. The hero directs the next step.
