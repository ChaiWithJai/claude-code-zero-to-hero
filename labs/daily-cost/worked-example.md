# A handout you can use today

A finished job has an output you can check. For this exercise, the job is turning teaching notes into a useful handout. Your sidekick drafts and organizes; you keep the teaching judgment. [NOTE-03]

## Predict

Write down your guess for API-equivalent usage and active review minutes. Then run `node cost-lab demo` at the repository root. For the interactive visual, use `node cost-lab serve` and open the printed localhost URL.

## Inspect

The supplied fixture is simulated. It contains three request estimates: Draft $0.00450, Check $0.00120, Repair $0.00105. Together they are $0.00675 at the bundled rates. Eight active minutes at an assumed $60/hour gives $8 of attention value. None of this establishes an actual invoice.

For included subscription usage, keep the API-equivalent figure separate from the fixed monthly fee. Dividing a plan fee across active days is an allocation, not a new charge. [NOTE-01]

Input, output, cache writes and cache reads use different prices. Haiku 5.5 changes its pricing tier above 100,000 input tokens per request, including cached input. [NOTE-02]

## Try one change

Add one repair in the visual. Predict the new usage before looking. Then increase active review by five minutes. Which change matters more at your chosen time value? Explain why.

## Feedback

The extra simulated repair adds $0.00105, for $0.00780. Five extra active minutes at $60/hour adds $5 of attention value. Your own hourly assumption may differ. A cheaper response can still leave an expensive unfinished job if it needs more review. [NOTE-03]

## Transfer

Choose one recurring task of your own. Name its output, retained judgment, acceptance check and stop rule. Record failed attempts as well as the accepted result. Preserve unknown usage instead of guessing. [NOTE-03]

Sources: the three supplied input notes and their official links. All dollar examples above are educational assumptions.
