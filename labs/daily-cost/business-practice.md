# What could Claude Code change in my business?

Claude Code is an AI assistant that can work with files and tools on your computer. A daily driver means a tool you reach for in ordinary work. No programming or pricing knowledge is required for this exercise.

Run `node cost-lab serve` at the repo root. Use the main story before opening technical tracing.

1. Choose **revenue** or **cost reduction**. Name a business outcome you would actually want.
2. Read the story assumptions. Predict what will change before changing a value.
3. For revenue, compare baseline and proposed conversion. Keep additional revenue, contribution and ongoing costs separate.
4. For cost reduction, compare active hours. Keep released capacity separate from money actually removed from a budget.
5. Expand the running-cost section. Name who builds the workflow, reviews exceptions and maintains it.
6. Find your industry in the optional 74-industry explorer. Follow the related Databricks source. The mappings and measurement ideas are authored adaptations, not a claim Databricks proved each GICS use case.
7. Choose a small pilot and a quality check. Use a comparable baseline or holdout and measure correction effort, margins and fulfillment ability.

## Direct your sidekick

If you choose to use your own agent, open `inputs/business-job-brief.md` and `inputs/revenue-pilot.csv`. Ask it to show assumptions and make an analysis you can inspect. It must not contact anyone. Running an agent consumes your allowance; the visual itself makes no model calls.

## Feedback on the synthetic case

Baseline conversion: 2%. Pilot conversion: 3%. Difference: one percentage point. At 1,000 equal opportunities that implies ten additional sales, $5,000 additional revenue and $3,000 contribution at 60% margin. Subtract $400 recurring run allowance for $2,600 recurring contribution after those costs. Keep the $2,000 one-time setup separate. Simple payback is about 0.77 months if the assumptions hold; no actual result or causal proof is claimed.

Cost case: forty active hours become sixteen. Twenty-four hours of capacity at an assumed $50/hour is $1,200 of time value. If payroll and contractor spending do not change, verified labor cash savings remain zero. With $100 new monthly running cost, recurring cash impact is negative $100 until a real cash reduction or a separate valuable use is established.
