## Make a wish you can explore

You are the hero in this course. The agent is support around your judgment, interests, and strengths. Begin with something you want to explore, not a list of everything a tool can do. A wish might be comparing three options, turning rough notes into a brief, or organizing material so you can think clearly.

Write three wishes. Choose one with a visible result you could inspect in a short sitting. “Improve my whole business” is too large for a first experiment. “Compare these three desk options against my budget and space” gives you a boundary. The boundary helps you learn; it does not limit what you may attempt later.

Your personal gravity map begins with the center: what matters to you, what you know, and what you want to keep deciding yourself. The rings describe support you may add. They are a teaching model, not a technical architecture or a measure of your worth.

## Read your decision patterns

Choose two low-stakes past decisions. For each, separate four things: what you felt before choosing, whose expectations influenced you, what you chose, and what happened afterward. Write “uncertain” when you do not remember. Do not rewrite your earlier feeling to make it match what happened.

Now look for a pattern. Perhaps you agree quickly to preserve harmony, or avoid a tool because the first screen feels unfamiliar. A pattern is a possibility to investigate, not a diagnosis. One good outcome does not prove an intuition reliable, and one bad outcome does not invalidate your strengths.

Use the reflection to design a next experiment. If you tend to overcommit, choose a task that fits a short sitting. If you hesitate until everything is understood, identify the minimum you need to begin. You may keep this page private.
